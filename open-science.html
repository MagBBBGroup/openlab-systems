OPEN SCIENCE · OUR VISION

Good scientific ideas deserve a longer life

Open science makes research more accessible, affordable and reproducible. We want to help researchers discover, build, adapt and maintain the experimental tools created by the scientific community.

WHY OPEN SCIENCE MATTERS

More research with fewer resources

Across the world, talented scientists develop ingenious instruments, software and experimental methods to answer questions that standard commercial equipment cannot address. When these designs are shared openly, other researchers can build on them rather than start from scratch.

Open hardware and software can make experiments possible with smaller budgets, enable more sampling sites or replicates, and give research teams the freedom to adapt equipment to their species, field conditions and scientific questions.

These benefits extend beyond individual laboratories: publicly funded research tools can continue generating scientific value when they are documented, reusable and maintained.

THE CHALLENGE

Sharing a design is only the beginning

Many excellent open-science projects are difficult to discover, reproduce or keep running over time.

01

Hard to find

Useful methods may be scattered across journal articles, supplementary files, personal websites and GitHub repositories, making it difficult to identify the right setup for a particular experiment.

02

Hard to reproduce

A publication may explain the scientific principle without providing every practical detail needed to source components, assemble the apparatus, configure software and validate performance.

03

Hard to maintain

Researchers often lack time, staff or funding to support a project after publication. Software dependencies change, electronics are discontinued, and code written for older computers or architectures may stop working.

OUR LONG-TERM AMBITION

A living repository of experimental setups

Not just an archive of old designs, but a resource that can be updated and used by new generations of researchers.

Alongside designing new instruments, we hope to develop and maintain a searchable collection of open scientific hardware and software for behavioural and ecological research.

Our aim is to bring together practical information that helps researchers build a system themselves, adapt it to their experiment, or ask us to help with its construction and testing.

We envisage a growing library of our own designs and, where permission and licensing allow, adapted or maintained versions of projects originally developed by other research groups.

This is a long-term goal, not a repository we claim to have completed today. Its scope and maintenance will depend on collaboration, available resources and sustainable support.

Build guides

Step-by-step assembly, wiring, setup, calibration and troubleshooting instructions.

Design files

CAD models, 3D-printable parts, schematics and component lists where sharing is permitted.

Working software

Code, configuration files, supported versions and updates for compatible modern hardware.

Research context

Original publications, authorship, licensing, applications, limitations and validation notes.

HOW WE WANT IT TO WORK

From a published idea to a usable research tool

1

Discover

Find a relevant method and the original research behind it.

2

Rebuild and adapt

Use documented components, current software and tested alternatives where necessary.

3

With appropriate permissions, contribute fixes, new configurations and lessons learned.

4

Keep it usable

Maintain clear version histories and update projects as technology evolves, when resources permit.

RESPECTING THE CREATORS

Built on collaboration and proper attribution

We want to preserve the contribution of the scientists who created these methods—not replace or obscure it. Every third-party project would identify its original creators, publications and applicable licences.

Before publishing or adapting third-party materials in our repository, we would seek the necessary permissions from their creators or other rights holders and comply with the applicable licences. Customer-commissioned designs would be published only when the customer agrees and all relevant rights allow it.

Our own experimental setups, guides and code will also form part of the collection, with updates and documentation shared as development continues.

A MORE SUSTAINABLE MODEL

Making research funding go further

Scientific instruments developed through research grants often represent substantial public investment. When those tools remain accessible and functional after the original project ends, they can benefit many more laboratories, students and citizen scientists.

Our ambition is to help extend that return on investment: reduce repeated engineering effort, make lower-cost research possible, and support the continued use of excellent ideas even when their original developers cannot maintain them.

Maintaining such a resource will require time, technical expertise and support. We hope to explore collaborations and sustainable funding models that make long-term maintenance possible.
